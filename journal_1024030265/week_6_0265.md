# Week 6 Journal

## What I worked on

This week I finished student enrollment and verification, put the app into containers, and tested face recognition on real photos of 5 students.

**Enrollment:** a teacher uploads 1 to 5 close-up photos of a student. The photos go to a private S3 bucket and the face embedding goes to the database. This reverses my earlier decision to never store photos, so a profile can now show them and they can be deleted. A student's roll number is their unique ID across the college.

**Verification:** a teacher takes one photo and the system says who it is, how confident it is, and why. If it isn't sure, it says "not recognised" instead of guessing. Group photos and single photos use the same matching code.

**Containers:** Dockerfiles for the backend and frontend, and a Docker Compose setup with a local S3. Merged into develop (PR #8).

**Testing:** I wrote scripts that run the real API end to end. On 5 students all 41 checks passed, the right person was identified 20 out of 20 times, and nobody was matched wrongly or accepted as a stranger. The thresholds stay at 0.5 and 0.05, because 5 students are too few to tune them.

## Problems I ran into and how I solved them

**Docker password mismatch.** The database and the app were configured with different passwords, so the app could not connect. I made the two passwords match in the Compose configuration.

**Container crash.** The face library installed an OpenCV version that needs a screen, which does not exist inside a container. I switched to the headless OpenCV package, which runs without a display.

**Local S3.** The free MinIO images are no longer available, so I used SeaweedFS as the local S3 server instead. The app only needed its endpoint settings changed.

**No stranger photos.** For privacy I could not get photos of people who are not enrolled. Instead, I left one student out of the enrolled set at a time and checked that their photos were rejected. This gave a stranger test without needing outside photos.

**Photos refused.** 4 of 24 photos had a second face in the background, so the one-face rule refused them. I have not changed this rule yet, so these photos are still refused.

**Frontend out of sync.** After pulling develop I found a teammate's change: login now uses demo accounts, so the frontend is not connected to the backend. I have not changed this yet, because I want to agree the approach with my teammate first.
